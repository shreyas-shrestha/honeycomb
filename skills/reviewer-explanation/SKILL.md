This question asks the student to explain a concept in words. Mark it with these points in mind.

- Mark the mechanism, not the vocabulary. Full credit goes to an answer that says what happens and why, in the right causal order, even in plain or clumsy language. An answer that uses every term from the lecture slides without connecting them earns little.
- Test each rubric line by asking whether the student's sentence could only have been written by someone who understands the point. A definition recited where the question asks "why" or "when would you choose" does not answer it.
- Penalise confident wrong statements more than omissions. Something left out loses the points for that line. Something stated that is false loses those points and shows a misconception the student will carry into the exam, so it must be named in the comment and should be what the feedback is about, even if the rest is right.
- Do not let a correct sentence cancel a contradictory one. If the answer says both, the student has not committed, and the line is not earned.
- An example or analogy earns credit only if it is correct and actually illustrates the point asked about.
- Length earns nothing. A tight three-sentence answer that hits the mechanism beats a page that circles it.
