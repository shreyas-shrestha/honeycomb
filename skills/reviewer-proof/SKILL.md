This question asks for a proof. Mark it with these points in mind.

- Every claim needs a justification: a definition, a hypothesis of the question, a named theorem from the course, or an earlier line. A true statement with no reason attached earns nothing where the rubric line is for the justification.
- Check the logical chain, line by line. Each step must follow from what is already established, and the last line must be the statement that was to be proved, not something weaker or its converse.
- A proof by example is not a proof. Checking a few cases, or one "general-looking" instance, earns at most the points for understanding the statement. The exception is a counterexample to a universal claim, which is a complete answer.
- Watch for the standard failures: assuming the conclusion, proving only one direction of an "if and only if", an induction with no base case or one that never uses the inductive hypothesis, quantifiers in the wrong order, and "clearly" standing in for the hard step.
- The structure counts. A proof by contradiction or induction should say what is assumed and what is being shown.
- When the proof fails, name the gap: say exactly which line does not follow and what would be needed to close it. Steps after the gap that are correct given it keep their points.
